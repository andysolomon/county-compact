# Issue #3 (CC-03) — Source map for period transport, terrain, settlements, and federal facilities

Probe date: 16 September 2026 (all HTTP codes and byte sizes below were observed that day with `curl -L`, generic browser user agent, no cookies). This document maps *where* evidence for 1942-02-01 Georgia can be obtained and under what terms. It records no findings about which roads, rails, crossings, terrain bands, facilities, or place names are correct for 1942; that is the Research half of CC-03. License text is quoted verbatim, never paraphrased. Coverage scale: **None** = nothing retrievable today for the target date; **Partial** = retrievable material brackets or approximates the date; **Full** = retrievable material directly covers the date.

## Executive summary

Of CC-03's six facets, none has a directly retrievable primary source dated 1942-02-01 today, but four are workable with bracketing sources and two are thin. **Roads: Partial** — David Rumsey road maps of Georgia dated 1939, 1940 and 1944 are downloadable through Internet Archive mirrors under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; no 1941–1943 edition surfaced, the GeorgiaInfo Historical Atlas of Georgia Counties (the source Issue #1 planned to use for the 1915 and 1952 highway maps) has been taken offline, and GDOT publishes no historical map library online. **Rail corridors: Partial/thin** — the only period-adjacent open map found is a 1934 Leahy's railway-distance map (Rumsey/IA); the 1940s *Official Guide of the Railways* is not on Internet Archive and HathiTrust blocks automated access; HAER holds structure-level records (e.g. a Western & Atlantic culvert in Marietta). **Rivers and crossings: Partial** — modern public-domain hydrography (USGS NHD) is downloadable for river geometry; period crossings depend on USGS historical topographic quads (topoView reachable, its product API failed today) and HAER bridge records; USACE reservoir pages returned 403. **Terrain classes: Full for physiography** — USGS Fenneman/Johnson 1946 physiographic divisions shapefile downloaded headers (public domain); wetlands only via modern USFWS NWI (Partial). **Federal facilities: Partial** — New Georgia Encyclopedia articles for every named site are online but are secondary and restricted (non-commercial fair use only); HABS/HAER has 458 Fort Benning records; the NARA catalog API returned an HTML shell and cannot be queried unauthenticated. **Settlement labels and seats: Partial** — the 1940 Census Vol. I Georgia chapters (public domain, 20–23 MB PDFs) and USGS GNIS Georgia names are downloadable; LOC Sanborn sets for Marietta (1923, 1948) and Savannah (1916, 1950) bracket the date but item-level rights metadata returned 503 and the Digital Library of Georgia is behind a Turnstile check.

## Newberry license regime (applies to any Georgia-archive-derived layer)

The caveat established in [issue-1-newberry-audit.md](issue-1-newberry-audit.md) §3–§4 governs every source above that is derived from Georgia archives (DLG, GALILEO, UGA, Georgia Archives) or that bundles a legacy license file. Quoted from that audit: "The strongest open statement is the Download pages' "reused for any lawful purpose, commercial or non-commercial, without licensing or permission fees to the library" together with the instruction that files containing "the original, out-of-date copyright license … can be ignored". That is a permission statement by the copyright holder, not a CC0 dedication." and "Do not relicense. Do not mark Newberry-derived data as CC0 or public domain." The Newberry Georgia page was re-checked today (HTTP 200, 12,492 bytes, unchanged). Extension for this source map: the Rumsey/IA road and rail maps carry an explicit CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 string with a separate "contact … for commercial use" instruction; NGE text is fair-use-only for non-commercial purposes. None of these is CC0; treat all three regimes (Newberry BY-NC-SA 2.5 or "any lawful purpose", Rumsey BY-NC-SA 3.0, NGE non-commercial fair use) as incompatible with commercial distribution until written confirmation is obtained.
